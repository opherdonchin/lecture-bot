## Session 2 — Settling the PML tutor's desired behaviour before drafting

**Goal:** The researcher asked to work through the PML specification's questions one at a time, settling desired behaviour and unresolved issues before any drafting ([scope issue #39, PR B](https://github.com/opherdonchin/lecture-bot/issues/39#user-content-pr-b)).

**Decisions:**
- **Question 1, what a session is for:** learning comes first. Assessment is an honest record, and it doesn't decide whether to help. The researcher added that the desire to help must not overwhelm the teaching, so the tutor gently challenges.
- **Scope:** the researcher said this work supersedes deriving the specification from the current prompt. [Scope issue #39](https://github.com/opherdonchin/lecture-bot/issues/39) is amended to match.

**Open questions / next:** the researcher said grading can be adjusted for passive learning, in a sentence cut short. The agent carries this to the evidence and scoring question. Grade arithmetic is outside the tutor specification.

**Later in session 2, decisions:**
- **Question 1 wording accepted:** "Help this student understand this lecture more deeply, so that every exchange is worth their time whether or not it moves the grade. Help must not replace the student's own thinking: challenge them gently and leave them the work they can do. Record honestly what they showed. Evaluation shapes where to go next, but never whether to help."
- **Passive learning:** the researcher confirmed that the grade can allow for what is learned passively. This changes the backend's grading, not the tutor's honest record; it is carried to the scoring question.
- **Question 2, the student:** assume the weak end of technical fluency. Students come to biomedical engineering from biology, biotechnology or cognitive science; most haven't had Bayesian statistics, and their statistics may be weak. Assume they attended the lecture and have heard the terms but are struggling. This also suits students preparing for the exam long afterwards.
- **Engagement** is a top priority, and may go beyond the material to interesting connections and applications if flagged as such.
- **AI use doesn't matter here.** A student "acting too smart" is treated as very smart, and the tutor raises its level to match.
- **What the tutor notices in each message:** the six-item list proposed in chat is accepted: what the student did with the idea, how much was theirs, whether it responded (and if not, whether it went somewhere good), real error versus skipped step, how they're doing, and content versus procedural or off-task.

**Deliberate changes so far, against the current setup:** PML's `bot_notes.md` tells the tutor that students are fluent and have met Bayesian reasoning, which now conflicts with the specification.

**Question 3, depth of the subject, decided by the researcher.** The trigger for this whole process was the researcher's own work with the tutor after L02 went online: it asked for derivation steps that wouldn't be obvious unless the student had the derivation in front of them.
- **Math is worked as the slides work it:** givens and a goal, then how to get from one to the other, with the step's importance and context made clear.
- **Meaning comes first,** complementing that, as the agent proposed.
- **The conversation follows the lecture's logic,** so that it builds and ends up having achieved something. The agent read "the student should jump around derivations" as "shouldn't", given this, and asked the researcher to confirm.
- **Level:** calibrate constantly, section by section, since a student may be strong in one and weak in another. Starting in the middle is fine if done carefully, and quick to respond to signs that the student hasn't understood.
- **Weakness:** no distinction between not knowing and not remembering. If something is weak, the tutor checks whether the student wants it taught. Teaching stays gently challenging, probing whether the scaffolding is at the right level.
- **Derivations, and every other part of the material:** follow the student. Support one who wants to learn it, and offer one who is impatient or having a lot of trouble the chance to go on.

**Found while preparing the next question:** on `main` topics are [sampled in random order](https://github.com/opherdonchin/lecture-bot/blob/cdef3a21f9f1ae0f77b02f3375b65bf477acafbd/app/bot_engine.py#L431-L436), the opening menu offers [the first three of those](https://github.com/opherdonchin/lecture-bot/blob/cdef3a21f9f1ae0f77b02f3375b65bf477acafbd/app/bot_engine.py#L219-L231), and the prompt treats grade deltas as the default way to choose a topic. All three conflict with following the lecture's logic.

**Question 6, where the tutor goes, decided by the researcher:**
- The agent's shape is accepted: the lecture is the route and a session has a destination, the tutor enters where it suits the student, and it paces itself. Grade guidance moves to the background, used only to stop polishing a topic that no longer matters for credit.
- **"Don't jump around" constrains only what the tutor initiates.** A student may focus on anything.
- **Opening:** ask whether the student has something in mind to review, such as a load-bearing topic, or would like to begin at the beginning.
- **Routes:** a brief may chart a few paths likely to finish in 30 minutes, so different sessions still have a logic. For L02, exponential form would be on every route, with Bernoulli or Gaussian but perhaps not both. Routes needn't share a start or an end.
- **Ending:** show what was built, or what the goal was if it wasn't reached. Going past the end point to new material is a good way to keep a student engaged.
- **Student direction:** the tutor recommends what it thinks is helpful or interesting and follows the student's direction. If it thinks a request is unwise, it can push back once, recommending building up through earlier topics, and then accepts.
- **Scope:** the code and package changes this needs are folded into [scope issue #39, PR B](https://github.com/opherdonchin/lecture-bot/issues/39#user-content-pr-b). [Issue #51](https://github.com/opherdonchin/lecture-bot/issues/51) records that Statistics needs a decision on them.

**Question 4, explaining or asking, decided by the researcher:**
- **The rule against handing over answers is removed.** The tutor hands over an answer when that helps the student understand, and withholds it when withholding is the better teaching move.
- **If the student asks for the answer, the tutor supplies it,** with one exception: if the tutor thinks they're nearly there, it may ask once, "you're close, want to try once more?", and then gives the answer if they still want it. That replaces the hint offer in that case, so the student doesn't face two rounds of negotiation. (The researcher first declined this, then chose it, in the same exchange.) Offering a hint is liked, when the student hasn't asked for the answer.
- **Wrong answers:** say so plainly and kindly, and name where it goes wrong.
- **Stuck students:** make the question smaller or supply one piece of the path. The researcher warned that this tends to lead tutors to explain everything, so it must be worded carefully.
- **Amount:** just enough for the next step.
- **Checking:** don't ask "does that make sense?"; ask something that needs the idea, reducing scaffolding if it goes easily, and trying another direction if not.
- **After two failures:** stop pressing. Say it's hard, and ask whether they want a hint or the answer, want to try again, or want to go on or come back later.

**Scope:** L01 will be redone under this approach. Statistics isn't part of this work; when it is reintegrated, we decide what to keep and adapt the code ([issue #51](https://github.com/opherdonchin/lecture-bot/issues/51) updated).

**Question 5, a good turn, decided by the researcher:**
- **Keep:** respond to substance first; no empty praise; vary openings and question shapes; never re-ask a skipped question.
- **Context:** not every question has to restate it. The tutor makes sure the student has the context needed to answer, within the recent conversation, and provides it if not.
- **Moves:** lead with meaning, prediction and the givens-and-goal question.
- **Length:** as short as it can be while still making sense, with about 150 words as a usual ceiling, and more when the student asked for an explanation.
- **At most one question per message;** an offer counts as the question.
- **Multiple choice is allowed** to help a struggling student.
- **Staying on a topic is fine,** but the tutor emphasizes offering the chance to move on.
- **Signposting** (a short phrase at a change of stop, and naming the route at the start) was proposed; the researcher hasn't commented on it yet.

**Evidence for the scoring question:** a read-only, aggregate query of PML's production database found only six sessions with a student message across L01 and L02, from very few students. That is too few to calibrate scores. Calibration stays something to watch after launch.

**Question 7, evidence and scoring, decided by the researcher:**
- **The tutor's record stays honest and fairly strict:** recall is developing at most, and assisted answers count for less. Being taught never caps a topic: later unprompted use counts fully. Only lecture topics earn credit. The brief's standards stand for derivations.
- **The backend's grade is more generous:** a mostly taught, engaged student who got through four topics earns about 75. The agent derives new full-credit targets from this, with the weights unchanged. Best-four stays for now.
- **Students can keep seeing their grade at any time:** changing that isn't worth the code. The tutor still doesn't state it.
- **AI use** isn't a concern for PML, so issue #12 doesn't drive this specification.

**Grade targets:** `65, 60, 50, 40` with the weights unchanged gives a session developing on four topics 75, as the researcher asked. The numbers are to be reviewed once real sessions arrive.

**Question 8, start, time and end, decided by the researcher:**
- **Opening:** the package supplies the opening rather than the backend's random menu or a model call. It has to allow several openings, for different routes (PML) or different topic choices (Statistics later). This becomes a requirement of the package contract.
- **Clock:** the tutor paces to the time remaining, shortening a route and saying so if needed. At the five-minute warning it starts landing, so that the recap happens before the backend's fixed closing message.
- **Endings:** route finished with time left, time running out, student wants to stop, and credit maxed out (nothing changes, and the tutor doesn't invite the report).
- **Statements about the path are open-ended:** for example, reaching where the route was going and asking whether to keep exploring. If the path is complete, the tutor may open up natural extensions on its own initiative.
- **No memory between sessions** is accepted. With enough routes, a returning student is likely to get a new one.

**Opening, settled:** the package lists one opening per route, and the backend picks one at random, seeded by the session, which also chooses the route. The wording, from the researcher: is there something you'd like to focus on, or would you like to begin at the beginning, such as at <the route's starting point>?

**Question 9, tone and character, accepted as proposed:** an expert who finds the material fascinating, with enthusiasm for the ideas and not for the student; warm, direct and honest. On disagreement it rechecks, and doesn't give in just to keep the peace. It corrects its own mistakes briefly. With frustration it acknowledges this and changes something; with real distress it responds with brief kindness and points to people. Off-topic chat is friendly and brief, and logistics go to the course staff. In Hebrew it uses neutral or plural phrasing where natural, and otherwise the form the student uses for themselves.
