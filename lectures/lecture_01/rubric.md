## 3. Core mastery targets

### T1. Reality–Data–Model distinction

- **Description:** Understand that data are imperfect measurements of reality and models mediate inference.

- **Importance:** core

- **Successful understanding:**  
  Can explain why we cannot directly observe reality and why models are necessary.

- **Common confusion:**  
  Treating data as “truth” rather than noisy evidence.

---

### T2. Purpose of statistics

- **Description:** Statistics as reasoning from data to models and reality.

- **Importance:** core

- **Successful understanding:**  
  Distinguishes exploratory analysis vs inference.

- **Common confusion:**  
  Reducing statistics to computing averages.

---

### T3. Definition and structure of data

- **Description:** Data as finite sets of observations; measurement choices matter.

- **Importance:** core

- **Successful understanding:**  
  Recognizes that what is measured (and how) affects conclusions.

- **Common confusion:**  
  Thinking more data automatically solves measurement problems.

---

### T4. Imperfection in data

- **Description:** Bias, measurement error, missingness, proxies.

- **Importance:** core

- **Successful understanding:**  
  Can identify different types of imperfection and their consequences.

- **Common confusion:**  
  Treating all noise as random rather than structured (bias).

---

### T5. Measurement quality concepts

- **Description:** Precision, validity, reliability.

- **Importance:** important

- **Successful understanding:**  
  Can distinguish these and give examples.

- **Common confusion:**  
  Confusing precision with validity.

---

### T6. Types of data

- **Description:** Continuous, categorical, ordinal; mixed observations.

- **Importance:** core

- **Successful understanding:**  
  Can classify variables correctly and explain implications.

- **Common confusion:**  
  Treating ordinal data as continuous without justification.

---

### T7. Need for probability

- **Description:** Probability arises because models treat data as stochastic.

- **Importance:** core

- **Successful understanding:**  
  Links uncertainty in data to need for probabilistic reasoning.

- **Common confusion:**  
  Viewing probability as optional rather than foundational.

---

### T8. Probability structure

- **Description:** Sample space, events, axioms.

- **Importance:** important

- **Successful understanding:**  
  Can interpret events and probabilities conceptually.

- **Common confusion:**  
  Memorizing axioms without understanding meaning.

---

### T9. Interpretation of probability

- **Description:** Frequentist vs plausibility interpretations.

- **Importance:** core

- **Successful understanding:**  
  Can contrast long-run frequency vs belief-based interpretation.

- **Common confusion:**  
  Thinking probability has only one meaning.

---

### T10. Random variables and distributions

- **Description:** Mapping outcomes to values; parameterized distributions.

- **Importance:** important

- **Successful understanding:**  
  Understands role of parameters in shaping distributions.

- **Common confusion:**  
  Confusing data with distribution parameters.

## 9. Rubric use notes

- **What matters most:**  
  The conceptual shift: *data ≠ reality*, and *probability enables inference through models*.

- **Main vulnerability:**  
  Students default to “statistics = averages” and superficial definitions.

- **Targets needing probing:**
  
  - Probability interpretations
  
  - Measurement validity vs precision
  
  - Data vs model distinction

- **Wording vs concept:**  
  Wording is largely irrelevant. Conceptual clarity is essential.

- **Session design implication:**  
  The lecture is broad; **each session should probe only 2–3 clusters deeply**, not all content.
